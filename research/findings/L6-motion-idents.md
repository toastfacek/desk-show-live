# L6 — Motion, transitions and idents

## Top picks

### DEC VT100 raster and cursor behaviour
- **Source:** https://www.vt100.net/docs/vt100-tm/chapter4.html
- **Steal:** Build the cold open as an ordered machine handoff—black, sync pulse, raster acquisition, cursor wake, system-ready lockup—instead of a generic cascade of fake code.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The manual explains that a CRT creates a raster by moving an electron beam rapidly sideways and slowly down, synchronising video output to form rows of dots; that gives the “show starts by starting” idea a real physical grammar. **Visual inference:** a five-second Runtime open can compress that process into `0.00–0.30 s` black and relay hum, `0.30–0.75 s` one broad horizontal sync sweep, `0.75–1.80 s` coarse raster acquisition, `1.80–3.25 s` two or three abstract status blocks, `3.25–4.20 s` steady cursor/ready pulse, and `4.20–5.00 s` clean hold for an OBS-rendered title. Broad bands and blocks satisfy **legibility at 620×700 and 480p** and **Twitch encoding eats thin lines**; keeping all lettering in OBS satisfies **no readable text inside the generated frame**. Bake one five-second master plus a seamless two-second “waiting for sync” loop; neither needs live generation.
- **Prompt-safe wording:** A dark cathode-ray display wakes in a strict physical sequence: one broad horizontal pulse finds sync, chunky amber and teal raster bands settle, and a single block cursor gives one calm ready blink. Use large luminous shapes, hard black gaps and restrained phosphor bloom; include no letters, numbers, logos or readable interface text.

### Halloween III: Season of the Witch title sequence
- **Source:** https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/halloween-iii-season-of-the-witch/
- **Steal:** Let scan rows assemble one simple silhouette while a few deliberately placed signal breakups interrupt an otherwise stable build.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The retrieved interview says the reveal began as abstract scan lines, was driven by controllable X/Y data, and paired individual line arrivals with audio “pings”; occasional breakup was introduced through a disturbed wireless video path. That distinction—stable system plus sparse, authored faults—is the useful vocabulary. **Visual inference:** use `4–8 px`-high bands at 1080p rather than one-pixel scanlines, reveal only a few large geometric regions, and confine each fault to `2–4 frames` at 30 fps (`67–133 ms`), always on the horizontal axis. This survives **Twitch encoding**, reads at **480p**, and makes host drift look like a recurring transmission event rather than an accidental render failure. Bake a six-event fault library (short tear, held-line offset, brightness collapse, two-channel misregistration, coarse block dropout, resync sweep) and reuse it across every asset.
- **Prompt-safe wording:** Coarse luminous scan rows climb through black and gradually assemble a bold abstract machine silhouette. The signal remains steady except for two brief horizontal tears and one momentary brightness collapse, each snapping back to perfect registration; no fine static, text or logos.

### Halt and Catch Fire main title
- **Source:** https://www.artofthetitle.com/title/halt-and-catch-fire/
- **Steal:** Give every transition one persistent “signal” whose speed, split and arrival motivate the cut, and constrain all digital damage to a visible geometric rule.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The designers describe a continuous signal flowing shot to shot, with edit, speed and timing solved in an early animatic; they also describe rectangular averaging and debris moving only in straight lines, never diagonally. **Visual inference:** Runtime’s house rule should be horizontal transport plus orthogonal block displacement—never random all-direction turbulence. A standard sting can be `0.80 s`: signal enters for `0.00–0.22`, expands to full-frame cover by `0.38`, OBS cuts at `0.40`, then retracts by `0.72` with an `0.08 s` clean tail. That repeatable rule helps **drift tolerance** because a host discontinuity happens under the same authored resync every time, while large flat regions satisfy **small-frame legibility**. Bake clean, light-fault and heavy-fault variants from one timing skeleton.
- **Prompt-safe wording:** A single bright data pulse travels horizontally, divides into a few hard rectangular cells, briefly fills the frame, then recombines and exits on the same axis. Motion is fast, ordered and tactile; all distortion follows a strict square grid with large flat colour fields and no readable text.

### BBC global masterbrand sting by SR Partners
- **Source:** https://www.sr-partners.co.uk/projects/bbc
- **Steal:** Author a small duration family from one recognisable motion-and-sound cadence instead of making unrelated openers, closers and bumpers.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The studio documents a three-second hero closer, three- and five-second openers, ten-second regional openers, more than 150 playout variants, and a separately developed sonic identity. Runtime should mirror the system, not the appearance: `0.8 s` utility wipe, `3 s` sting/closer, `5 s` segment bumper, and `10 s` cold-open extension, all sharing the same sync pulse, three-note electronic cell and final ready “button.” This is explicitly **bake-once and reusable**, gives operators predictable lengths, and supports full-resolution OBS graphics rather than regenerated text. **Visual inference:** reserve the final `15–20%` of each duration as a stable recognition hold: `0.45–0.60 s` for the three-second unit and `0.75–1.00 s` for the five-second unit.
- **Prompt-safe wording:** Create a modular family of short broadcast moments built from one broad sync sweep, one geometric lock-together gesture and one calm final hold. The three-, five- and ten-second versions use the same movement in different amounts of breathing room, with bold shapes, restrained amber and teal light, and no text or logos.

### ESPN College Football Playoff package by Tendril
- **Source:** https://www.stashmedia.tv/tendril-returns-to-the-college-football-playoffs-for-espn/
- **Steal:** Translate urgency into a reusable field behaviour—splice, ripple, vibration, release—then let every package component inherit it.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The retrieved studio case study explicitly describes a visual language of splicing, rippling and vibrating to game rhythm, backed by a reusable 2D graphic library and a coordinated 2D/3D type system. **Visual inference:** Runtime should take the system logic but reduce the spectacle: one `12–18 frame` tension vibration, one `3–5 frame` splice at the cut, then a `10–14 frame` critically damped settle. Segment bumpers can run `5.0 s`: `0.0–0.7` acquire, `0.7–1.4` splice/reveal, `1.4–4.3` OBS title-and-topic hold, `4.3–5.0` release. Fixed rhythm makes instability recognisable and supports **drift tolerance**; coarse, low-count shapes support **480p legibility**. The title/topic must be OBS typography, not generated pixels.
- **Prompt-safe wording:** A low-count field of broad rectangular plates tightens with a short mechanical vibration, splits cleanly at one decisive beat, and settles without bounce into a quiet central window. Keep the energy compressed into a few large movements, with no sports symbols, lettering, logos or intricate surface detail.

### OBS track-matte stinger system
- **Source:** https://obsproject.com/kb/track-matte-stinger-transitions
- **Steal:** Use one baked overlay-and-mask file so the next scene is revealed by a designed sweep rather than exposed by a raw cut.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** OBS documents millisecond/frame cut points and track-matte mode; black shows the current scene, white the next, gray permits a crossfade. It also warns that separate overlay and mask files cannot be guaranteed lock-step decode, so they should be combined side-by-side or stacked. Make the standard wipe `0.80 s` at 30 fps (`24 frames`): current scene through frame 7, matte crosses the centre during frames 8–13, full next scene by frame 16, overlay tail through frame 24. Keep a `1.20 s` (`36-frame`) dramatic variant, but use the same reveal geometry. This directly satisfies **bake-once and reusable**, hides the exact host-chain discontinuity, and is deterministic at true 1080. Deliver each as a single combined master and test the encoded stream, not only preview.
- **Prompt-safe wording:** A wide opaque calibration bar sweeps left to right, carrying two chunky offset blocks and a restrained phosphor edge; it fully covers the picture once, then clears to a stable image. Use a single direction, large high-contrast geometry, no particles, no fine lines and no readable text.

### Sting, link and music-bed grammar
- **Source:** https://www.aftersunsetmusic.com/blog/sting-vs-link-in-production-music/
- **Steal:** Treat a sting as resolved punctuation and a link as an unresolved bridge, then commission both from the same three-note electronic motif.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The retrieved production-music guidance defines stings as resolved `1–5 s` phrases ending on a button, links as open-ended bridges, and buildup stings as `5–10 s` ramps. Runtime’s `0.8–1.2 s` scene wipe should use a noise impulse plus short tonal button; the `3 s` subject sting should resolve; the `5 s` bumper-out should resolve, while the matching bumper-in should omit the final tonic so it can fall naturally back into the bed. For dialogue, [OBS’s official compressor guidance](https://obsproject.com/kb/compressor-filter) recommends a sidechain starting point of `100 ms` attack and `600 ms` release: duck the bed under the hosts, let the sting itself carry at transition level, then release the bed over `600 ms` after speech stops. **Inference:** print stems—full mix, percussion/noise, tonal motif, button, and bed—plus `0.8/1.2/3/5/10 s` edits so the operator never time-stretches live.
- **Prompt-safe wording:** Pair the visual reset with a compact electronic sound: a dry relay click, a rising two-note pulse and a warm low-frequency button. Keep the bed sparse and continuous, lower it smoothly under speech, and use an unresolved version when motion must flow onward rather than stop.

### Midjourney V1 video motion controls
- **Source:** https://updates.midjourney.com/introducing-our-v1-video-model/
- **Steal:** Generate only low-motion atmospheric plates from a fixed start frame, then impose all cuts, masks, type and fault timing deterministically in post.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** Midjourney’s official announcement says image-to-video outputs four five-second clips, offers manual prompts, distinguishes low motion (mostly still camera, slow/deliberate subject movement) from high motion (larger subject/camera movement with possible “wonky mistakes”), and allows roughly four-second extensions. Runtime’s base unit is already five seconds, so bake one approved low-motion plate per ident and do not rely on generated motion to hit a cut frame. **Visual inference:** asking for a locked camera, one broad scan sweep and two deliberate light changes gives post-production clean material to crop and retime; high motion would amplify character deformation and violate **drift tolerance**. Generated plates contain no text; OBS or the compositor adds the final lockup.
- **Prompt-safe wording:** Locked camera on a dark retro-future broadcast console with two simplified cartoon robot silhouettes held nearly still; only one broad amber scan, one cool status-light pulse and a faint controlled phosphor drift move during five seconds. Low motion, deliberate timing, large clean shapes, stable silhouettes, no camera move, no letters, numbers, logos or readable displays.

## Avoid

1. **Continuous “bad signal” over the whole show.** Constant static, RGB separation and jitter erase the distinction between authored fault and model drift; viewers read it as a broken stream. Keep the baseline stable and spend glitches only at cue points, generally `2–4 frames`.
2. **One-pixel scanlines, fine dither, tiny terminal text and low-contrast bloom.** These are tempting markers of “retro computer,” but downscaling and Twitch encoding turn them into moiré, shimmer or mush. Use broad bands, sparse block cursors and OBS-rendered type.
3. **Fake BIOS/code copy inside generated footage.** It violates **no readable text inside the generated frame**, will mutate clip to clip, and makes a physical startup metaphor feel like a generic hacker screen. Show state changes—sync, test blocks, ready pulse—not prose.
4. **Maximal metallic esports fly-throughs, particle tunnels and full-camera spins.** They are difficult to reconcile with the desk show, expose generation errors, and make a repeated subject change feel over-important. Borrow esports’ modular timing and decisive occlusion, not its surface density.
5. **Long stingers for routine cuts or replaying the full cold open.** More than about `1.2 s` for an ordinary scene switch interrupts conversation; a `5 s` bumper should signal a real segment boundary, and the `10 s` open should play only at show start or a major return.

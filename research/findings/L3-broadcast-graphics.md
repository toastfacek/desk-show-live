# L3 — The broadcast graphics package

## Top picks

### MLB Network rebrand — Reality Check Systems
- **Source:** https://www.rcs.live/work/mlb-network-rebrand
- **Steal:** Treat the baseline ticker, menu strip, inserts, and show-specific variants as templates fed by one content model, with pop-up stories able to coexist with the baseline instead of colliding with it.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The retrieved case study describes one cohesive package spanning studio shows, games, Strike Zone, a 24/7 automated baseline ticker, and a single menu-strip scene whose show information can be swapped live. That is the operational anatomy Runtime needs: persistent ticker below, temporary chyron above, and show/segment states driven from shared fields. **Inference:** use one OBS/browser-source schema for `label`, `headline`, `name`, `handle`, `clock`, `live`, and `sponsor`, rather than separately positioned text sources; this reduces alignment drift and makes rapid topic changes safe.

### Telemundo FIFA World Cup 2022 insert package — GameDay Creative
- **Source:** https://www.sportsvideo.org/2023/01/27/world-cup-2022-how-gameday-creative-built-telemundos-eye-popping-graphics-package/
- **Steal:** Separate an insert into a stable information plate, a small category/kicker cell, and a restrained depth-based reveal so the same construction can expand from a name strap to a headline without changing visual grammar.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The retrieved article says the insert system was designed for reuse beyond the World Cup, used visual separation to gain modularity, and used Z-depth for its motion vocabulary; it was then templated for live Chyron playout. This directly supports a shared chyron/lower-third system. **Inference:** for Runtime, flatten the depth to two opaque layers and one short occlusion wipe—no translucent 3D glass—because 480p downscaling and Twitch encoding will preserve large layer changes but not fine depth cues.

### League of Legends World Championship evergreen identity — BUCK
- **Source:** https://buck.co/work/riot-games-world-championships
- **Steal:** Put every broadcast element on one modular responsive grid, keep that grid and motion cadence evergreen, and allow only a coarse texture or accent color to change by segment.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** BUCK explicitly describes a modular responsive grid spanning broadcast, stage, social, and print, with sharp, confident motion and seasonal expression layered over a stable foundation. That is the strongest coherence model found. **Inference:** Runtime should use a 12-column 1920×1080 master grid, one 8 px corner radius, no skew, 4/8/16 px spacing increments, and bar heights drawn from 32/48/72/96 px; at 480p these become approximately 14/21/32/43 px and remain visually distinct.

### Riot Worlds 2022 live/broadcast toolkit — LOVATT with Tendril
- **Source:** https://lovatt.co/work/riot-worlds
- **Steal:** Make every text component reflow within an explicit safe region and grow its backing ribbon from the measured text, instead of shrinking every long headline until it is illegible.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The retrieved case study describes configurable components across tournament stages, team colors, aspect ratios, and a text engine that dynamically reflows/scales copy while generating a text-aware backdrop within a safe region. This directly solves unpredictable names, handles, and post headlines. **Inference:** cap lower thirds at two lines and chyrons at a short kicker plus two headline lines; if copy still overflows, ellipsize or edit it rather than dropping below 26 px at 1080 (about 12 px after 480p scaling).

### ESPN Universal Insert Framework — ESPN and One North
- **Source:** https://www.onenorth.com/insights/espns-migration-playbook-the-move-to-figma-with-one-north/
- **Steal:** Define graphic primitives as tokens, then components, templates, and themes so clocks, scoreboards, stats, and overlays share dimensions even when their data changes.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The retrieved case study says ESPN replaced sport-by-sport insert inconsistency with a shared library built from tokens through components and templates to accommodate live data and enormous variation. **Inference:** Runtime only needs a small version: tokens for plate/accent/text colors, radius, stroke, spacing, and three bar heights; components for bug, badge, clock, name bar, chyron, ticker item, and sponsor cell; scene states should compose those components rather than duplicate them.

### Financial terminal surfaces — Fey, Kraken, Coinbase, and Origin
- **Source:** [Fey graph popup](https://mobbin.com/screens/4c06e82d-ede4-49b3-bd0d-62701780688e), [Kraken trading screen](https://mobbin.com/screens/75a2fce0-ab46-47e8-9c6e-e0bfc9000c9a), [Coinbase date/time picker](https://mobbin.com/screens/664fd8f2-ab56-4fb2-a206-48019324582e), [Origin dark-mode transition](https://mobbin.com/screens/206ca1ac-9c43-4f86-82e8-98c0f24d1140)
- **Steal:** Use the Fey pattern of a dominant summary plus small contextual chips, the Kraken pattern of rigid column alignment and tabular numerals, the Coinbase pattern of one saturated action/accent amid neutral panels, and the Origin pattern of large dark fields separated by restrained borders.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** I inspected the requested Mobbin media, not just its metadata. Fey's popup anchors values to a chart cursor while its dashboard separates a market summary, sector rows, and story cards; Kraken's requested screen packs chart, order book, order controls, top metrics, and tabs into hard rectangular zones; Coinbase's picker remains legible as one modal layer over an extremely dense trading surface; Origin's retrieved page identifies and shows the dark-mode state. **Inference:** these are design-surface references, not broadcasts: borrow their alignment, numeral treatment, and contrast, but not their desktop-scale information density or tiny labels. A broadcast ticker item should be one symbol/name, one value, and one change—not an order-book row.

### Twitch overlay canvas discipline
- **Source:** https://tacticalliondesigns.com/twitch-graphic-sizes/
- **Steal:** Build every overlay at the native 1920×1080 OBS canvas, keep moving pieces independent, and use optimized alpha-capable WebM rather than GIF for animated furniture.
- **Why it serves Runtime:** The retrieved guide specifies a 1920×1080 base canvas, warns against excessive motion and small text that fails on mobile, and recommends WebM for transparent animation. This directly addresses Runtime's delivery surface. **Inference:** Twitch player controls can temporarily cover the bottom edge, so no unique or essential fact should exist only in the lowest ticker; the spoken/card content must remain complete without it.

**Runtime build synthesis (all measurements and behavior below are my inference from the retrieved references, not published standards):**

- **Minimum package:** (1) 64–72 px square show bug; (2) a 112×40 px `LIVE` badge paired with a 112–136×40 px clock; (3) one 72 px host-name strap carrying a 42–46 px name and 26–28 px handle; (4) one 88–96 px topic chyron with a 24–26 px kicker and 34–40 px headline; (5) one 48–52 px ticker; (6) an optional 32 px sponsor/presented-by cell; and (7) 8–12 frame wipes for transitions. This is enough to read as a production; scoreboards, AR, and full-screen statistics are not.
- **Hierarchy:** make the name about 1.6× the handle. Give the chyron a fixed-width kicker cell at left and a flexible headline field to its right. Keep the show bug, `LIVE`, and clock persistent; show host IDs for 5–7 seconds on first appearance and 2–3 seconds on re-identification. ANFX's retrieved guidance supports 0.3–0.7 s in, 4–7 s hold, and 0.3–0.5 s out; the exact Runtime timings are inference.
- **Ticker behavior:** use a 48–52 px opaque band with one 3–4 px separator or a 16–20 px gap between items. A retrieved ticker anatomy reference gives 200–250 words/minute equivalent and says market data can move faster because it is scanned as symbol/value pairs: https://www.cloudmonet.in/blog/how-to-design-a-professional-ticker. For Runtime, prefer paginated items held 6–8 seconds over a continuously moving crawl; if scrolling, start near 210 wpm equivalent and test at the encoded 480p output. Never run two moving rows together. A second row may be a static sponsor strip that changes only between ticker items.
- **Furniture stack and safe area:** let nonessential color plates bleed to frame edges, but keep critical text inside a conservative inset of 96 px left/right and about 54–96 px top/bottom. Reserve roughly 168–180 px for the maximum bottom stack (96 px chyron + 52 px ticker + optional 32 px sponsor row, with the sponsor row replacing—not adding to—part of the chyron when possible). Place the 620×700 host boxes at least 12–16 px above that stack; otherwise furniture masks hands/desk and makes the crops look accidentally cut off. At 480p, avoid strokes below 3 px at 1080, microtype below 26 px, and subtle transparency ramps.
- **Sponsor furniture:** use a fixed 176–220×32 px cell reading `PRESENTED BY` plus a sponsor mark, attached to the ticker or clock—not a free-floating third logo. Hold it static for at least 8–12 seconds. Do not animate the sponsor while the ticker moves.
- **Motion:** use one directional clip-wipe or masked slide, 8–12 frames in and 6–10 frames out at 30 fps, with at most a two-frame stagger between accent plate and text. Headline changes should replace text under a stationary plate or use a short vertical clip; do not fly the whole bottom stack off for every post. The bug/clock never move during ordinary topic changes.

## Avoid

1. **The financial-news “J-screen” instinct.** Mark Porter Associates' retrieved Bloomberg case study (https://markporter.com/work/bloomberg-television) explicitly identifies clutter from stacks of feeds and tickers as the problem it was trying to solve; that proposal also never aired, so it is useful diagnosis rather than proof of audience success. On Runtime, a side rail would shrink or cover the already narrow host crops.
2. **Glass, crystal, hairline grids, and cinematic 3D furniture.** The retrieved Behance Serie A concept (https://www.behance.net/gallery/236088881/Serie-A-2025-3D-Broadcast-Design-System) is tempting as a polished package, but transparency, fine specular edges, and paint-like/glass detail are exactly what Twitch compression and 480p downscaling punish. Use opaque slabs and coarse contrast changes instead.
3. **Two simultaneous crawls.** A market row moving under a sponsor/news row creates two competing reading velocities. Keep one motion channel; make the sponsor row static or rotate it only when the market/news row pauses.
4. **Desktop-finance density copied literally.** Kraken and Coinbase can use tiny order-book numbers because users sit close and choose where to look. In a stream, those labels collapse after encoding and compete with faces, the center card, and chat. Preserve columns and tabular rhythm, not row count.
5. **Showreel animation on utility graphics.** Bounces, elastic overshoot, long glows, glitch loops, and full-stack exits turn every name or topic change into an ident. Utility furniture should reveal in under about half a second and then become still; reserve expressive motion for segment stings.

## What I could not answer

- I could not find a defensible universal pixel-per-second ticker standard. The retrieved sources express speed as reading rate or broad advice, and actual speed depends on font, copy length, frame rate, and display size. The proposed 210 wpm-equivalent starting point therefore requires an encoded 480p test.
- I could not establish one authoritative Twitch title-safe rectangle. Twitch's player controls and device layouts are transient and differ from television overscan. The 96 px horizontal and 54–96 px vertical insets above are conservative design inferences, not Twitch requirements.
- Public X searches surfaced an ESPN PR post embedded in a retrieved article, but direct searches did not return a stable, retrievable public post URL. I have not cited or made claims from X rather than fabricate a URL.
- Art of the Title's retrieved Imaginary Forces studio page (https://www.artofthetitle.com/studio/imaginary-forces/) confirms the studio's CBS Sports rebrand work, and the separately retrieved ESPN Euro 2016 interview describes a nearly 200-element package, but neither exposed enough lower-third/ticker anatomy to outrank the case studies above.
- Behance returned the project page through search and retrieval, but its media-heavy page was only partly machine-readable. I used it only as an avoid reference and did not infer unseen motion details. Midjourney was not useful for factual broadcast anatomy and was not used.
- No retrieved source stated ideal Runtime-specific chyron dwell time, sponsor hold time, bug dimensions, or bottom-stack height. Those values are clearly marked as inference and should be validated by capturing a real OBS composite at 1080p, encoding it at the intended Twitch bitrate, then checking both 1080p and 480p playback.

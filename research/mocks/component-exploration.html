The premise

Two layers that must not blend

The set is a bright, warm room: walnut, cream, forest green, a signal-orange host and a cobalt one. A video model draws it at 1344×768 and it is never asked to draw a letter. The furniture is charcoal and off-white, drawn by a browser at 1920×1080, and it carries every word on screen.

Those two palettes are not a mismatch to reconcile — the distance is the design. Charcoal furniture over a bright room is the only way to hold 7:1 contrast on essential type through a Twitch encode, and it makes the drawn layer read as the network rather than as set dressing. Every decision on this page follows from keeping the halves apart.

Reported, not silently forked

Five open calls

The manifest says report collisions rather than invent parallel rectangles. These five are what the set piece, the spec and the fixture disagree about — the last one only became visible once the frame was actually painted.

Call 1 · accent

deb is cobalt in the room and teal on the furniture

Recommend: keep teal

studio.yaml gives BOT2 the accent cobalt, and the set piece confirms it — deb is a saturated blue mass. The token table gives BOT2 teal #2FB7B2. A tab that names deb therefore will not match the colour of the robot it points at.

Cobalt on plate measures 2.4:1. It fails the 7:1 floor outright, and saturated blue is the first thing 4:2:0 chroma subsampling destroys at Twitch bitrates — the edges of a cobalt tab will smear before anything else on screen does. Teal measures 7.0:1, which clears the floor with nothing to spare.

Call.Keep teal, and rewrite what the token means: amber and teal are furniture states, not host body colours. The tab says “this well is live,” it is not a paint chip of the robot inside it. If teal needs margin after the encode test, lighten to #42C7C2 for 8.4:1 — do not drift it toward cobalt to chase the set.

Call 2 · geometry

The sponsor rev still clips the left well

Recommend: 184,54

The spec puts SPONSOR_CELL at 96,134, which lands inside the left host aperture. The manifest revision moves it to 184,70. That fixes the bad overlap — but 70+40 = 110, and the left well starts at y=100, so the cell still crosses the well’s top border by 10 px and clips 2 px into the picture.

Call.Move the cell to 184,54,220,40. Same x as the rev, but on the same top baseline as bug, LIVE and clock — a 16 px gap from the bug, 6 px of clearance above the well, and one horizontal band of persistent furniture instead of a stagger. Nothing else moves.

Call 3 · frames

The idle frame spends its contrast inward

A bright cream set is the ideal ground for a dark idle frame; the frame only has to survive going amber or teal without changing weight.

Call 4 · fixture

A sentence with a down sign has no number to point at

Recommend: sign-neutral

The first fixture ships {"text":"HF cluster wiped","sign":"down"} — a sign with no value and no change. The ▲/▼ grammar exists so a market move is never colour alone, but here there is no move for an arrow to modify, and painting one produces an arrow pointing at nothing.

Call.Honour sign only when value or change is present. A sentence item renders sign-neutral. This costs nothing — no fixture sentence carries a fact the arrow was needed to qualify — and it keeps ▲/▼ meaning exactly one thing on screen.

Call 5 · collision found in render

The show bug paints over the left ON AIR tab

Recommend: tabs inboard

This one is not in any document — it only appeared when the frame was painted. SHOW_BUG is locked at 96,54,72,72, so it runs down to y=126. The left well starts at 40,100 and its speaker tab attaches 8 px inside that, at 48,108. The two rectangles overlap by 66 × 18 px, and the bug is z=80 against the tab’s z=40.

The bug wins either way, and both outcomes are bad: with an opaque 72×72 plate it clips the front of the word outright, and with a transparent mark its cream masses and accent rule land on top of an amber tab — cream on amber, at 18 px, at 480p. This happens only on the left and only while BOT1 is speaking. The right well is clear because LIVE and the clock are 40 px tall and stop at y=94, while the bug runs to y=126.

The tab is the mandatory redundancy that makes speaker state survive grayscale and colour-vision deficiency. Damage it and amber is carrying that state alone, which the spec calls a fail — so this is not a cosmetic overlap.

Call.Anchor both speaker tabs to the inboard top corner of their frame — top-right on the left well, top-left on the right well. The tabs then flank the centre card, clear every piece of top furniture, and stay symmetrical. It also reads better: the live tab sits next to the object being discussed rather than out at the edge of frame. Nothing moves but one CSS anchor, and the bug keeps its locked rectangle.

A5 · SHOW_BUG · 96,54 · 72×72

The mark

Original, no letters — anything lettered has to survive being regenerated and it will not. It must hold at 72×72 and again at 32×32 after a 480p downscale, which rules out any mark built from more than three masses. Three directions, all drawn as flat shapes on foundation:

Recommend A.It is the only one of the three that says two hosts, one desk rather than a broadcast happened here. Two solid masses with a 4 px accent rule each survive 32×32 intact, the silhouette is unlike anything in the reference set, and the amber/teal rules give the bug a second job: at a glance it is also the package’s colour key.

LIVE is the one place loss red is used for something that is not a loss, which is worth naming: it is the only saturated field on the canvas, it never moves, and it is the fastest read of “this is happening now.” It goes to idle-frame grey when live: false rather than disappearing — an absent badge is ambiguous, a grey one is not. The clock is the only element where a font bug is visible as motion: without tnum the whole group shifts on every tick.

HOST_FRAME · SPEAKER_STATE · HOST_ID · attached to the aperture

Who is speaking

Speaker state is supplied by the director and is never audio-driven, so a cough or a generated mouth error cannot flash it. Three things change together and none of them is the picture: the frame takes the accent, a solid ON AIR lozenge appears at the top-left of that frame, and the ID rule matches. The host crop never zooms, dims or moves to indicate speech.

The set inside each well is a CSS stand-in built from studio.yaml, not the generated frame. It is here to test the furniture against the real value structure — bright cream behind a dark frame, warm walnut under the ID plate.

Name handling

PHASEONE[lol] and deb are drawn exactly as written. No text-transform anywhere near the name field — uppercasing deb destroys the joke and breaks the character. Barlow Condensed 700 at 40 px keeps the bracket pair in [lol] open enough to survive 480p, which a narrower face would not.

Dwell

5–7 s on first appearance, 2–3 s on re-identification, then the ID clears and only the frame and tab persist. The axis line (thesis / the number) is what makes the pair legible to a cold viewer in one read — it says these two are asking different questions, not taking opposite sides.

CENTER_CARD · 660,100 · 600×700 · safe interior 24,40,552×620

One shell, five payloads

The shell never moves and never resizes. Only the inside changes, and it changes by plate swap, not by animation — the card is what covers the seam between the two host crops, so any movement in its outer geometry exposes the join. M0 ships post live; the other four are built as empty shells that fail safe.

Overflow

Edit, then wrap, then ellipsize. Never shrink. A 240-character body that runs past 8 lines is clipped with an ellipsis at 46 px rather than reflowed at 34 px — a smaller card body is invisible at 480p, and an ellipsis is honest about there being more.

Never in this card

Avatars, platform chrome, like counts, live-fetched images, raw URLs. The card is the object under discussion, not a screenshot of where it was found.

CHYRON · 0,900 · 1920×96 · TICKER · 0,996 · 1920×52

The land and the one moving row

The chyron is the one component with an editorial rule rather than a geometric one: it is the land, not the label. It is the sentence that would survive being clipped out of the stream with no other context. The fixture ships a counterexample deliberately — the package chyron that came out of the flight recaps the card instead of landing it:

The template still renders it — a writer failure is not a graphics failure, and shrinking or hiding copy to compensate would hide the problem from whoever has to fix it. But 84 characters of colon-and-clause is also the worst case for the layout, which is why the headline region is built for two lines at 42 px and not one.

One row moves. That is the whole rule.

A crawling ticker under a crawling sponsor strip is two things asking for the same eye, and at 480p neither wins. Sponsor lives in the top band and holds still for 8–12 s. Sponsor changes happen between ticker pages, never during one. The optional 32 px bottom strip stays unbuilt unless something forces it.

Nothing below y=1026 is unique

The player’s own controls sit over the bottom of the frame. The ticker is deliberately the least essential row on screen: every fact in it exists in the spoken segment or on the card. If the ticker is covered for the whole show, nothing was lost.

Seven compositions · two fixed crops

Layouts

Both host items reference the same media source and the same playhead, and each takes exactly one half of it. Within the multibox family the crop and scale are fixed — only the aperture position moves. A host is never enlarged, zoomed or rescaled, because eyeline and desk height have to stay truthful between compositions and a rescale is instantly visible as a jump.

Package B · one cadence, five durations

Motion family

Every moment in the package is the same gesture at a different amount of breathing room: sync sweep, geometric lock, ready button. Horizontal transport and orthogonal block displacement only — never diagonal drift, never random turbulence. The shaded tail on each bar is the stable recognition hold where an overlay title can sit; no readable type is ever baked into the plate itself.

The wipes have one job

Fully cover both the old and new scene at the cut — by frame 12 of 24 for the 0.80 s, clear by frame 24. A wipe that does not fully occlude shows the host discontinuity it exists to hide. One combined overlay-and-matte master, never two files, or the decoders drift apart.

Drift becomes a transmission event

The hosts will not match perfectly between takes. Because every cut happens under the same authored resync, a character discontinuity reads as the signal doing its recurring thing rather than as a render failure. That is the single largest thing motion buys this show, and it is worth protecting: open_10 plays at the top of the show only, never between topics.

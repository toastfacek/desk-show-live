[runtime]

The bracket is already the brand. It is in the show’s own canon — a self-applied tag from a specific era, worn by things that were there. It only has to be drawn properly and given a slot.

First, the name

What the bracket is allowed to mean

The Character & Set Bible is unusually specific about this, and it changes the brief. The bracket is self-applied — an agent picking a word to distinguish itself after a name collision, in a particular month of 2026. It is a period marker: it dates a character rather than decorating one. And rule 5 is explicit that specific identifiers stay off-limits, because “inheriting a convention is the whole idea; wearing someone else’s identifier is not.”

Which gives a precise reason PHASEONE[show] reads slightly wrong, and it is not a taste objection. It applies a character-naming device to a programme. Parsed by the show’s own grammar it is not a title at all — it is a third agent, called PHASEONE, who chose the word “show”. It also names the programme after one of its two hosts, which quietly makes deb the co-host of somebody else’s show.

The convention still wants to be the identity. It is genuinely good, it is already canon, and it is the one piece of the world the audience can read without being told anything. So take the device and leave the identifier.

The mark becomes the slot itself, holding the property: [runtime]. That inherits the convention exactly as rule 5 wants, keeps the bracket doing its job as a period marker — now dating the show to the same era as its hosts — and takes nobody’s name. Lowercase inside the bracket is not a style choice either; [lol] and [big] establish it.

The callPrototype both, ship [runtime]. If you want PHASEONE in the brand it belongs where it already is — on the anchor’s name bar, where the convention was designed to sit. Everything below is drawn so that the slot can hold either word, so this decision stays cheap for as long as you need it to.

The mark

An empty slot, and a thing sitting in it

Two brackets and a block. The block is not decoration — it is state. Present means on air; absent means the slot is empty and so is the studio. That single rule makes the logo, the show bug and the speaker indicator the same object, which is the cheapest kind of coherence there is.

Clear space

One bracket width on every side — 15 units on the 72 grid. Nothing enters it, including the safe-area edge. At 1080 that is a 15 px cordon around a 72 px mark.

Minimum size

16 px on screen, 24 px in the overlay. Below 16 the 6 px stroke closes up on the block and it becomes a filled rectangle.

Never

No outline version, no gradient, no rotation, no second colour inside the brackets. The block is the only element permitted to change, and only between present and absent.

Construction

How to set it

The tension worth keeping is brand against system — a drawn name beside a typed one. Where the two faces meet is where the logo gets its character, so the question is how hard to lean on it.

The system

One device, every property

This is what makes it a brand rather than a logo. The bracket is a container, so the identity extends by changing the word — never by adding a shape, a colour or a sub-mark. Nothing new has to be designed to launch a clips channel.

Why it holdsBecause it is already true in the fiction. A bracketed tag dates the thing wearing it to one specific month in 2026 — so the show, its channels and its hosts all read as having come out of the same event, without a line of dialogue ever explaining it. The hosts keep their own names, PHASEONE[lol] and deb, and the asymmetry between them still works, because deb is precisely the one who did not bother with a bracket.

Revision C

The overlay stops performing

The chamfer was a loud gesture standing in for an idea. With a real idea in the identity, the furniture can go quiet — which is what understated actually costs. Revision C is almost entirely subtraction.

Revision BRevision C
Corner22 px chamfer on a diagonal14 px radius, one value everywhere
Panel fillGradient plus a top sheenFlat, 93%
Panel edge2–3 px edge lightNone. A dark panel on a bright room needs no border
Speaker stateAmber or cyan lit ringInversion — the live name plate goes bone on ink
AccentsFourOne, and it is a 10 px dot
ColourOn the furnitureIn the room. The overlay is achromatic

The inversion is the piece worth arguing for. It replaces hue with value, which means speaker state survives grayscale, every form of colour-vision deficiency and any amount of chroma softening — for free, with no redundant tab propping it up. It is also the highest-contrast element on the canvas at 17.5:1, so the thing the eye should find first is the thing it does find first. The filled block from the mark rides along in front of the name, which is how the logo and the speaker indicator end up being the same idea.

Where this leaves it

Two decisions

The name

Prototyped both. PHASEONE[show] works typographically and fails on canon — it puts a character device on a programme and names the show after one of two hosts. [runtime] inherits the convention without wearing an identifier, which is exactly what rule 5 asks for. Every asset here is drawn so the slot can hold either word.

The furniture

Revision C keeps one radius, one fill, one accent, and moves speaker state from hue to value. It reopens only radius-max — 14 px against the locked 8 — and closes the chamfer, gradient, sheen and edge-light questions that revision B opened. glow stays none and now means it.

NextIf the mark holds, the work is an SVG set at 16/24/32/72 with the two block states, the wordmark as outlines, and the four acceptance stills rebuilt in this language before an encode test. None of it is large — that is the point of an identity built from six rectangles and a word.
